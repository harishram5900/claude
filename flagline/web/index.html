    The gap Flagline fills

    The National Weather Service already forecasts wet-bulb globe temperature (WBGT), the heat measure sports medicine uses. But a forecast grid is not a practice plan. Coaches still have to look it up, convert it to their state's flag table, decide what that means for pads, breaks and practice length, and keep a written log.

    Flagline does that for every public high school in the country, using the NWS forecast where it is issued and our own engine where it is not.

    How we checked the numbers

    Our WBGT engine reproduces the NWS official WBGT forecast to within 0.7°F on average across 1,464 forecast hours in 8 cities, with almost no bias (−0.25°F). Flag thresholds follow the Korey Stringer Institute regional guidelines; the Category 2 table matches the Delaware DIAA 2025 policy exactly.

    Who buys

    About 17,500 US public high schools run outdoor sports. Heat rules are spreading: Texas's UIL requires WBGT monitoring starting in 2026, and many states already require WBGT readings and written logs. Small and rural schools without a full-time athletic trainer are the ones least able to buy hardware and most likely to need the plan.

    Sources and limits

    Data

    • National Weather Service gridpoint forecasts (api.weather.gov): official WBGT, HeatRisk, temperature, dew point, wind, sky cover.
    • NCES EDGE Public School Locations, 2024–25: 17,558 high schools.
    • NASA POWER hourly (T2M, RH2M, WS2M, all-sky solar) for the national analysis; Open-Meteo historical weather for each school's summers.
    • Flag thresholds: Korey Stringer Institute regional guidelines (Grundstein et al., 2015); activity rules adapted from the Delaware DIAA Weather Policy, Fall 2025.
    • Preseason plan: Casa et al., Preseason heat-acclimatization guidelines for secondary school athletics, J Athl Train 2009.
    • Deaths: Sports Health 2024 (NCCSIR 1982–2022); NFHS citing NCCSIR.

    Limits

    • Forecasts describe a ~2.5 km grid cell, not your field. Turf, shade and wind change WBGT on the spot. Always take the required on-field reading.
    • Default heat categories are by state; confirm the category your state association uses.
    • Flagline supports, and does not replace, your athletic trainer and your state's heat policy.
