How it works

Four clinical tests, rebuilt for the phone in your pocket.

Each one is based on a test neurologists already use in the clinic. Kinora times every touch, every point of your drawing and every vibration of the phone, then compares you with your own baseline.

  1. 01 Finger tapping

    Alternate between two targets for 10 seconds per hand. Adapted from the MDS-UPDRS finger-tapping item.

    Measures
    Taps per second, rhythm variability, slowing over the 10 s, accuracy

  2. 02 Spiral drawing

    Trace an Archimedean spiral from the centre out, the standard bedside tremor test.

    Measures
    Distance from the guide, tremor size and frequency (3.5–8 Hz), drawing speed

  3. 03 Rest tremor

    Rest the hand holding your phone on your lap for 10 seconds. The accelerometer records every tiny movement.

    Measures
    Tremor size (m/s²), peak frequency, share in the 3.5–7.5 Hz rest-tremor band

  4. 04 Sustained voice

    Say "aaah" for 5 seconds. Parkinson's often makes the voice softer and less steady.

    Measures
    Pitch steadiness, loudness steadiness, voice clarity (harmonics-to-noise ratio)

The part a clinic visit can't see

Every check is tagged with time since your last dose.

Over a few weeks, Kinora shows how your tapping speed rises after levodopa and fades before the next dose: the "wearing-off" pattern neurologists adjust medication for, drawn from your own data instead of memory.

Evidence

Built on published research, tested line by line.

Smartphone tapping tracks motor severity

A tapping app's results correlated with motor UPDRS and bradykinesia scores in 57 people with Parkinson's and 87 controls (Kim et al., PLoS ONE, 2016). A second study found significant correlations with MDS-UPDRS Part III (r = 0.28–0.61) and good repeatability (Lee et al., Journal of Parkinson's Disease, 2016).

Voice carries a motor signal

Measures of a sustained vowel separated people with Parkinson's from controls and suited remote monitoring (Little et al., IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering, 2009).

The spiral is the bedside standard

Archimedean spiral drawing has been used to rate tremor severity for decades (Bain & Findley, Journal of Neurology, Neurosurgery & Psychiatry, 1993). Kinora measures the same drawing to the pixel and the millisecond.

Our signal checks

  • 5 Hz tremor in a spiral → found at 5 Hz
  • Clean spiral → error under 1%, no tremor
  • 5 Hz phone shake → peak at 5 Hz
  • Still phone → near-zero tremor
  • Steady 140 Hz vowel → 140 Hz, HNR > 15 dB
  • Wobbling vowel → over 3× pitch instability
  • Slowing taps → decrement detected
  • Steady 5 taps/s → 5 taps/s (±0.3)
