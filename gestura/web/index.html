How it works

Tested on people it had never seen.

Every model runs in your browser. Before putting each one in front of you, we measured it on signers and hands it was never trained on. These are the real numbers.

From camera to voice

  1. See the hands. Google MediaPipe finds 21 points on each hand, plus the body and face, about 30 times a second.
  2. Read the letter. A neural network trained on more than 10,000 real hand photos and webcam recordings of the ASL alphabet turns the 21 points into a letter. Optional calibration learns your own hand in a minute.
  3. Read the sign. For whole words, the winning model from Google's Isolated Sign Language Recognition competition describes the motion of both hands, arms and face; a classifier we trained on 11,000 recordings of Deaf and hearing signers (ASL Citizen) names the word.
  4. Make it English. ASL has its own word order. Common sequences become natural sentences: BATHROOM WHERE becomes "Where is the bathroom?". Fingerspelled words are checked against the 10,000 most common English words.
  5. Speak. Your device's own voice reads the sentence aloud.

Limits we want you to know

  • ASL is a full language with its own grammar. Gestura recognises fingerspelling and 340 individual signs and joins common phrases; it does not translate free-flowing ASL conversation yet.
  • J and Z involve motion; Gestura reads their final handshape.
  • Good light, your upper body in frame and a short pause between signs make a big difference.

Data and models

  • Hand, body and face tracking: Google MediaPipe Tasks (Apache 2.0).
  • Sign features: 1st-place solution, Google Isolated Sign Language Recognition (Kaggle), MIT licence.
  • Word training and testing, teaching animations and test videos: ASL Citizen (Desai et al., 2023, Microsoft Research), recorded by Deaf and hard-of-hearing community signers; official signer-independent split. Used for non-commercial education.
  • Alphabet: ASL alphabet photos (Hugging Face, Marxulia v03), ASLNow! webcam landmarks (MIT), EitanG98 ASL letters (Unlicense); outside test: Keithsel/asl_signs.
  • Word list: Google 10,000 most common English words.
